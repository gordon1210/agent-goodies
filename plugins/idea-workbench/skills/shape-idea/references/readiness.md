# Idea brief readiness

Use this gate to distinguish a useful draft from a brief that is ready for the user to approve. Do not treat completeness of the template as proof of readiness.

## Classify statements consistently

- **Fact:** A directly observed or reliably sourced statement. Record the source.
- **Assumption:** A temporary planning premise accepted despite incomplete evidence. Record the consequence if it is wrong.
- **Hypothesis:** A falsifiable belief about behavior, value, or feasibility. State what evidence would support or refute it.
- **Decision:** An explicit choice among tradeoffs. Record the rationale and decision owner.
- **Open question:** An unresolved issue. Record why it matters, how it can be answered, and whether it blocks the next stage.

Do not use confident wording to hide uncertainty. A repeated assumption remains an assumption.

## Check the brief

### Problem and outcome

- Identify the people or system behavior affected.
- Describe the current situation and its cost or limitation.
- Separate evidence of the problem from beliefs about it.
- State why the idea is worth considering now.
- Describe an outcome rather than merely restating a requested feature.
- Make success observable without inventing unsupported targets.

### Scope and appetite

- Limit the work to one meaningful, coherent slice.
- State the acceptable investment, complexity, and risk closely enough to reject unsuitable approaches later.
- Define in-scope behavior, non-goals, constraints, and behavior that must not regress.
- Resolve conflicting boundaries or identify them as blockers.

### Uncertainty and risk

- Label every material unknown as an assumption, hypothesis, risk, or open question.
- Surface likely rabbit holes, dependencies, and irreversible choices.
- Give each high-impact unknown a practical way to investigate or contain it.
- Allow non-blocking unknowns to remain only when they do not prevent honest option comparison.

### Artifact integrity

- Keep facts, assumptions, hypotheses, decisions, and questions in their own sections.
- Cite sources precisely enough that another agent can verify material facts.
- Remove contradictions, empty promises, and accidental solution commitments.
- Make the brief understandable without relying on unstated conversation context.

## Assign status

- **Draft:** One or more blocking checks fail, or the user is still resolving the framing. State the blockers and continue with one question at a time.
- **Ready for approval:** All blocking checks pass and remaining unknowns have an owner or investigation path. Present the full brief for review.
- **Approved:** The user explicitly accepts the written brief. Record the approval; do not infer it from silence or enthusiasm about the general idea.

Approval is the gate to option exploration. A draft that feels plausible is not a substitute for approval.
