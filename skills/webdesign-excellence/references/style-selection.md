# Style selection without aesthetic contamination

**Load when:** no primary direction exists or the user explicitly asks to explore alternatives.

## Decision order

Honor an explicit style or supplied brand first. For an existing UI, preserve its
visual grammar unless a redesign is requested. Otherwise choose by communication
job, content, asset quality, and interaction frequency—not by the word “modern.”
The names below are this skill's practical taxonomy, not objective trend rankings.

| Direction | Choose when | Distinctive mechanism | Weak fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editorial | Content, culture, studio work | Type-led hierarchy and paced image scale | Repeated dense operational tasks |
| Technical | Infrastructure, tooling, precision | Structural lines and credible product evidence | Decorative fake telemetry |
| Product-led | Everyday SaaS and task flows | Clear hierarchy, useful UI, restrained surfaces | A campaign that needs theatrical spectacle |
| Quiet luxury | Craft, hospitality, premium objects | Material quality and spacious restraint | Missing original assets or obscure purchasing |
| Organic | Human-centered, tactile, warm identity | Art-directed imagery and soft material cues | Low-contrast beige as the whole concept |
| Expressive graphic | Culture, events, bold campaigns | Controlled color blocking and graphic typography | Form-heavy daily operations |
| Neo-brutalist | Direct, assertive, deliberately raw brand | Hard edges, offset planes, candid hierarchy | Accidental unfinished styling |
| Cinematic | A story genuinely led by moving imagery | One narrative frame and controlled pacing | Slow media masking an unclear offer |
| Atmospheric | Abstract technology or emotional ambiance | Rich environment behind quiet foreground | Glow on every component |
| Glass | Layered navigation over rich content | Translucent chrome with opaque content | Text-heavy nested transparent cards |
| Retro-futurist | Explicit period or genre reference | One coherent era vocabulary | A claim that “retro” is universally modern |
| Immersive spatial | Products or stories that benefit from space | Meaningful object, camera, or scene interaction | Unnecessary rendering infrastructure |

Open the chosen `style-*.md` from SKILL.md. Do not load every dossier for comparison.
If two plausible options remain and the user requested exploration, present two
separate concept descriptions or prototypes. Alternatives are not a hybrid.

## Style versus technique

A grid, a serif, rounded corners, bento composition, a scroll spy, and grayscale are
not mutually exclusive styles. They are mechanisms. Apply one when it supports the
chosen grammar. Shared techniques are not a reason to import another style dossier.

Do not mandate a three-font stack, square corners, monochrome, or asymmetry across
all jobs. Conventional cards and symmetrical layouts are correct when the content
is genuinely comparable. Anti-generic means intentional, not unfamiliar at any cost.

## Deliberate hybrids

Only combine directions when the request or communication problem warrants it.
Name the primary owner of type, palette, shape, layout, imagery, and motion. Give
the secondary direction a bounded region or role. Example: a technical product page
with an editorial case-study interlude can retain one type and color system while
changing reading measure and image scale. “Half luxury, half cyberpunk” is not a contract.

Treat a brand family with separate campaign microsites as scoped systems; consistency
does not mean every surface has identical density. When rules conflict, resolve the
ownership before implementation. Accessibility and truthful behavior are never style options.

**Exit:** one selected direction, or an explicitly scoped hybrid; a reason for the
choice; two or three rejected treatments; no requirement to use all matching techniques.
