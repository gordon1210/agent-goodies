# Style: contemporary editorial

**Load when:** editorial is the chosen primary direction, not merely because a serif appears.

## Design thesis

Let the arrangement of words and images create identity. The page should feel edited:
some ideas dominate, others form a quiet reading layer. A publication, architecture
practice, cultural project, or studio case study can benefit from this direction.

## Grammar to establish

Pick a clear alignment spine and a measured reading column. Use a display voice with
strong scale contrast and a calm text voice; these may be the same family. Let image
size follow editorial importance. Captions, credits, dates, and section labels should
form a stable secondary rhythm, not a swarm of tiny decorations.

Decide whether color comes primarily from photography or from the graphic system.
Both are valid; do not accidentally use a different strategy in each section. Use
whitespace to separate ideas and fine rules to mark real boundaries. A single
oversized word can act as structure when the surrounding content gives it room.

## Concrete composition

For a studio case study, begin with title, a brief proposition, and one decisive
project image. Follow with a readable account of constraints beside supporting
artwork, then a contrasting full-width detail. End with outcome evidence and a
next-project link. The precise sequence follows the story; it is not a reusable
six-section mandate.

Set deliberate line breaks only after testing actual text and widths. Cropped
photography may extend beyond the reading measure; meaningful text must not.
Asymmetry should still share baselines or edges. Optical margin corrections are
preferable to arbitrary rotations of every card.

## Optional techniques and boundaries

Image masks, clipped display reveals, or sticky captions can reinforce the reading
rhythm. Start with none and select the relevant technique only for a real need.
Do not import cursor replacement, all-caps body copy, 9px metadata, or long hover
delays because a reference prompt contains them. A serif/sans/mono combination is
an option, not a rule. Rounded image treatments can be intentional here.

**Avoid:** identical teaser cards for unrelated stories; every line at display size;
low-contrast captions; unexplained technical status labels; animation required to
read a heading. Do not borrow industrial grid ornament simply to make the page look technical.

## Adaptation and acceptance

On narrow screens, preserve the reading order, widen the text measure relative to
the viewport, reduce type dominance, and choose stronger image crops. Replace
pinned compositions with a readable sequence where needed. At rest and with motion
off, the hierarchy and editorial pacing should remain obvious.

**Pass:** the page has a distinct typographic hierarchy, a coherent image story,
and useful reading rhythm without depending on fashionable effects.
