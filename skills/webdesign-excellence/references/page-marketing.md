# Page route: marketing, landing pages, and services

**Load when:** the site explains an offer and should lead to an informed next action.

## Structure around questions, not template sections

Identify the primary audience and what they need to decide. Explain what is offered,
who it is for, the useful outcome, the supporting evidence, and the next step.
A hero, trust strip, three cards, testimonials, pricing, and FAQ are not mandatory
sections. Include a section only when it answers a real question or objection.

Choose one primary action for a given decision stage. Secondary actions can serve
a different readiness level, such as viewing a product example before booking a
call. Make button labels describe the next event. Do not make every action equally
loud or require unnecessary account creation to read basic information.

## Proof as composition

Place credible evidence near the claim it supports. Product behavior, real outcomes,
actual work, a useful comparison, and clear constraints are stronger than fabricated
logos or generic praise. Draft placeholders must remain visibly provisional; never
publish invented users, growth percentages, or testimonials.

For SaaS, a representative product slice often explains more than a decorative
object. For a service business, a case study and an understandable process may be
better. For a physical product, show relevant details and context. The chosen style
should support this content rather than dictate an unrelated visual world.

## Conversion without manipulation

Make pricing units, commitments, recurring terms, and important limitations clear
where applicable. State what happens after submission and which fields are really
needed. Do not introduce fake urgency, default opt-ins, hidden costs, or obscured
cancellation. Never claim a style change improves conversion without evidence.

Use headings with actual meaning and credible copy. Replace vague “unlock the
future” language with the product's real capability. Keep essential content in
semantic HTML, with appropriate title, description, heading hierarchy, and crawlable
links. Structured data must describe real page content, not invented ratings.

## Acceptance

Can a new reader explain the offer from the first meaningful view? Is the proof
specific? Does the primary action have an honest destination and expected outcome?
Does the mobile page preserve the same information rather than just the sales claim?
Test forms, navigation, image loading, and page speed—not only the hero screenshot.

If measurement is in scope, define a hypothesis and authorized instrumentation.
Distinguish a usability observation from an actual conversion result.
