# Technique: analyzing a supplied motion reference

**Load when:** a supplied recording, animation, or accessible live reference should
inform web behavior. Not required for motion designed directly from a brief.

## Resolve the operation

Distinguish **integrate the actual media** ([existing video](technique-video.md)),
**reconstruct selected behavior** in DOM/SVG/3D, and **take loose inspiration**.
A video attachment alone selects none of these. Use the request and existing context;
ask one focused question only if the intended operation still materially differs.
Reconstruction does not authorize embedding the source, downloading private media,
copying assets, or reproducing unsupported product claims. Apply [asset rights](assets.md).

## Inspect evidence, then interpret

View actual accessible media. A title, thumbnail, or description is insufficient
evidence of movement. If unavailable, record the missing evidence and work on
independent static layout; do not claim inspection. With available approved local
tools, inspect metadata and extract frames into task-owned scratch space, retaining
the source. Scrub densely before, during, and after transitions and watch continuous
playback. Uniformly spaced contact sheets alone cannot establish smoothness.

Record a compact observation table with source timestamp/range, framing and relative
screen coverage, stable anchors, depth/occlusion, movement direction, transition
mechanism, readable hold, and ending. Distinguish camera motion from object motion
where visible cues support it; mark uncertainty otherwise. Estimate timing only
when the source does not expose reliable timestamps. Mark hidden/offscreen behavior
as unobserved rather than completing a plausible story.

Follow one subject through a transition: what stayed fixed, what changed scale,
what crossed in front, what disappeared behind a mask, and where attention landed?
Inspect typography at readable resolution. A silhouette does not establish actual
geometry; apparent exploded parts do not prove technical internals, measurements,
or interactive behavior. Record visible appearance without inventing those facts.

## Translate into an agreed web target

Separate four columns in the working notes:

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Observed | A ring stays near the lower third while two labels exchange positions |
| Preserved invariant | The ring remains the identity anchor through the handoff |
| Intentional adaptation | Narrow screens place both labels below the ring without travel |
| Acceptance | Anchor remains visible; labels never collide; result is readable at rest |

Turn the selected mechanism into a [beat map](technique-choreography.md), using
the current page's primary style and actual content. Source timestamps and shot
counts are descriptive, not binding after an approved structural adaptation.
Evaluate against the agreed target, removing superseded criteria instead of
testing contradictory historical defaults. Exact brand assets and claims require
their own supplied or approved evidence.

**Failure → remedy:** copied surface effects without meaning → name the information
handoff; strained morph → preserve one anchor and cut the rest; unsupported camera
inference → offer an explicitly interpreted path; inaccessible source → request
the essential asset and mark analysis incomplete; too much source choreography →
retain only the mechanism serving this page.

**Verify:** compare selected source and output states plus continuous playback;
review wide/narrow/short adaptations, interruption, and reduced-motion equivalents.
Do not report a contact sheet as motion review or a visual resemblance as functional
equivalence. **Fallback:** an original static composition or a smaller explicitly
interpreted sequence, with the evidence limitation recorded.

This observation/adaptation procedure and its example are original heuristics.
Media timeline behavior is documented in [existing video](technique-video.md#sources).
