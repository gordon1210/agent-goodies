# Composition, hierarchy, and visual rhythm

**Load when:** a page feels generic, crowded, flat, or disconnected even before effects.

## Design the information silhouette

Order the content by the user's questions. Decide one dominant element per region,
a supporting element, and a quiet layer. This can be a product, a sentence, a price,
a data comparison, or a photograph. It need not be a giant heading.

Sketch the page in large rectangles. Alternate intensity intentionally: a dense
proof section can follow an open introduction, but changing background color every
section is not narrative. Make transitions follow content rather than a template quota.
Use a shared alignment spine so asymmetry reads as controlled.

## Geometric moves

Use unequal columns when the information is unequal. Test 7:5 or 8:4 as starting
relationships, not mandatory ratios. Align captions, metadata, and actions to
meaningful edges. Give images useful breathing room; do not fill every empty region.
For comparable items, equal geometry is often better than forced variation.

Choose a small spacing family and distinguish internal component spacing from
section rhythm. Optical correction is allowed: a circular icon may need a different
inset than a square. Document the reason rather than accumulating arbitrary values.
Avoid enclosing every fragment in its own bordered card. Group with proximity,
whitespace, background, or a shared divider before adding containers.

## Content integrity

Use the real headline before tuning line breaks. A mock heading with three short
words does not validate the layout for a long product name. Preserve DOM reading
order; avoid grid placement or flex ordering that makes keyboard navigation jump.
Cropping is for decorative imagery, not required text or purchase information.

Create page continuity through repeated details: a caption format, divider treatment,
a section index when useful, or a consistent image horizon. One recurring detail can
provide identity without adding new effects.

## Repair order

1. Remove competing focal points and unnecessary wrappers.
2. Correct width, measure, alignment, and content order.
3. Correct type scale, line wrapping, and whitespace.
4. Align media direction and controls.
5. Only then adjust shadows, gradients, texture, and movement.

**Checks:** squint at a screenshot; can you find the primary task? Hide decorative
layers; does the hierarchy survive? Read only headings; does the page make sense?
At narrow width, does the content have a designed order instead of a collapsed poster?
