# Style: restrained product-led design

**Load when:** the chosen identity should be clear, contemporary, and effective for repeated use.

## Design thesis

Let the product, useful content, and competent interaction create distinctiveness.
Restraint does not mean anonymous gray cards. Identity can come from a type rhythm,
a precise accent, a recognizable illustration language, and exceptionally clear states.

## Grammar to establish

Use a readable interface type system with a display role reserved for genuinely
expressive moments. Define surface levels and radius families by component role.
A button, data row, modal, and marketing illustration need not share one radius.
Keep borders and shadows coherent rather than adding every available elevation cue.

Use color to support hierarchy and state. Balance whitespace against task density;
operational tables should not become a marketing page. Retain enough contrast in
secondary labels that users can still work quickly. Establish icon size, text
alignment, field heights, and focus styling as a system.

## Concrete composition

A product marketing page can pair a focused claim with an annotated product view,
then explain a workflow through a few useful examples. Inside the application,
replace that display hierarchy with a stable navigation shell, clear page title,
contextual actions, and the content necessary to complete the current task.

Treat empty, pending, partial, failed, and successful states as designed surfaces.
A real example of the product doing its job is stronger than a row of abstract
feature icons. Keep demos visibly distinct from live operational information.

## Optional techniques and boundaries

Restrained elevation, responsive grids, subtle state transitions, and product
micro-demos fit naturally. A bento section is justified only if heterogeneous items
need that structure. It is not a compulsory brand motif across every page.

**Avoid:** generic purple gradients as a default; a different visual effect for
every feature; giant headlines in settings screens; ceremonial page entrances;
turning all data into detached cards; removing useful labels for minimalism.
Do not ban familiar layouts when familiarity helps the audience.

## Adaptation and acceptance

Adjust density, labels, and navigation around actual tasks. Do not simply hide
secondary controls on small screens without another route to them. Preserve
compare-and-scan behavior for tables and selection patterns for touch.

**Pass:** the most important task is obvious, controls feel consistent, and the
product has an identifiable visual voice without extra interaction cost.
