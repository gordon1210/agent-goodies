# Information Architecture, Flow & Narrative

Use this guide to decide what belongs, in what order, and in what form.

Structure should follow the user's decision or task. Visual variety cannot rescue an incoherent sequence.

## Start with the job of the surface

For each page, screen, or section, identify:

- audience state on entry
- question or task
- information required
- decision or action
- evidence needed
- next destination
- failure or exit path

Remove or combine surfaces that cannot justify a distinct job.

## Choose the experience mode

- **Persuade** — guide attention through a reasoned argument.
- **Operate** — support fast, repeatable tasks and state awareness.
- **Read** — optimize comprehension, navigation, and continuity.
- **Experience** — use pacing and atmosphere as part of the message.

A site may contain several modes, but each surface needs a dominant one.

## Persuasive narrative

Common jobs include:

- hook or orientation
- relevance and stakes
- concrete product or offer
- mechanism or process
- benefit or outcome
- proof
- comparison
- objection handling
- risk reduction
- action

Use only the jobs the audience requires. Do not turn the list into a mandatory section template.

A section is justified when it advances the argument. Label its job before choosing its visual form.

## Product flow

For operational interfaces, map:

- entry and prerequisites
- user decision points
- system responses
- branching and permissions
- error and recovery
- completion
- return and continuation

Prefer familiar patterns when innovation would create learning cost without meaningful benefit.

## Navigation and information architecture

- Name categories using audience language.
- Keep sibling items at comparable conceptual levels.
- Expose high-value destinations; do not hide them for visual minimalism.
- Separate global navigation, local navigation, context, and actions.
- Make current location and available next steps clear.
- Provide search when users retrieve known information at scale.
- Validate labels and grouping with representative users or evidence when stakes justify it.

## Section and layout diversity

Avoid mechanical repetition:

- identical heading stacks
- alternating image/text splits as the only rhythm
- endless three-card rows
- arbitrary bento cells
- a card around every idea
- uniform section heights regardless of content

Vary composition because the content job changes: demonstration, comparison, data, quote, process, editorial explanation, proof, or action.

One controlled signature moment can create identity. It should serve the thesis and not compete with every other section.

## Show the thing

Prefer:

- real interface capture
- annotated workflow
- comparison
- diagram
- data
- result
- before/after
- physical or contextual photography
- interactive demonstration

Use prose to orient and interpret the evidence, not to replace it.

## Responsive narrative

Mobile is a prioritization problem, not a column-stacking operation.

For each section decide:

- what must remain first
- what can collapse or defer
- how media crops
- how comparison remains understandable
- how controls reflow
- how long labels and translations fit
- whether interaction changes by input mode

Preserve meaning and task sequence even when composition changes.
