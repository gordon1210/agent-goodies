# Audience, Positioning & Offer

Use this guide when a website, page, campaign, or message needs a clear reason to exist and a specific reason to be chosen.

## Audience by situation

Define the audience through behavior and context:

- trigger that brought them here
- job or progress they seek
- current workaround or alternative
- cost of the current state
- desired outcome
- knowledge and awareness
- decision criteria
- objections and perceived risk
- authority and constraints
- language they already use

Avoid unsupported demographics and fictional persona details. A useful audience description changes the message, structure, or product decision.

## Positioning

A practical positioning statement answers:

```text
For <specific audience in a specific context>,
<product/offer> is the <category or frame>
that helps them <valuable outcome>
because <distinct mechanism or evidence>,
unlike <current alternative>.
```

This is a thinking tool, not necessarily homepage copy.

Check:

- Is the audience narrow enough to recognize themselves?
- Is the category understandable?
- Is the outcome important and observable?
- Is the mechanism or advantage credible?
- Does the difference matter to the decision?
- What does the position deliberately not claim?

## Offer anatomy

Define:

- core outcome
- exact deliverable or access
- mechanism
- effort required from the customer
- price or commitment
- time to first value when known
- eligibility or constraints
- risk reversal
- support or service level
- next step

Do not hide material constraints in fine print or compensate with vague superlatives.

## Awareness and source

Match the message to what the visitor already knows:

- **Unaware or problem-aware** — establish relevance and stakes before product detail.
- **Solution-aware** — explain the mechanism and category difference.
- **Product-aware** — prioritize proof, fit, switching, and risk.
- **High intent** — reduce friction and clarify the transaction.

Traffic from an ad, email, referral, comparison query, or product surface carries an expectation. The destination should continue the same promise and vocabulary.

## Message hierarchy

Build from:

1. main promise
2. audience relevance
3. mechanism
4. benefits
5. evidence
6. objections and constraints
7. action

A message map may include several supporting points, but the artifact still needs one primary promise.

## Proof plan

List the evidence available for each important claim:

| Claim | Evidence available | Strength | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | Case study, product demo, benchmark, customer quote | Strong/medium/weak | Missing detail |

Useful evidence includes:

- product demonstration
- specific customer outcome with permission
- methodology and data
- independent review or certification
- recognizable customer logo with permission
- transparent limitation
- guarantee or reversible first step

Visual polish is not proof. Never use fabricated metrics, fake names, borrowed logos, or unsupported security language.

## Objection map

For each major objection, decide whether to:

- answer it directly
- demonstrate the answer
- reduce the risk
- change the offer
- remove unnecessary friction
- acknowledge a limitation

Do not bury the hardest objection in a generic FAQ if it blocks the decision earlier.

## Evaluation

The position and offer are ready when another person can explain:

- who it is for
- what changes for them
- how it works
- why it is credible
- why it differs
- what they should do next
