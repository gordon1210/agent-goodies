# Conversion, Marketing Copy & Interface Writing

Use this guide for persuasive copy, CTA design, forms, UX writing, errors, empty states, and consistency of product language.

Clarity and credibility beat cleverness. Consistency beats unnecessary synonym variety.

## Conversion principles

- Keep one primary action per decision point.
- Match the promise to the visitor's source and awareness.
- Explain the offer before asking for commitment.
- Put proof beside the claim it supports.
- Address material risk where it arises.
- Remove fields, choices, and steps that do not serve the current decision.
- Preserve a lower-commitment path only when it is genuinely useful.
- Make the post-action outcome clear.

Conversion is not manipulation. Do not hide cost, preselect consent, fabricate urgency, make cancellation obscure, or use shame-based copy.

## Headline and lead

A headline should carry a useful claim or frame. A lead should clarify:

- what the product or offer is
- who it serves
- how the outcome is achieved
- any critical constraint

Useful structures include:

```text
<Outcome> without <cost or obstacle>
<Specific category> for <specific audience>
<Do the job> in <credible time or process>
<New capability>, enabled by <mechanism>
```

These are prompts, not formulas to force. Avoid vague verbs such as “transform,” “elevate,” or “unlock” when the sentence does not say what changes.

## Benefit and evidence

Write:

```text
Benefit — mechanism, evidence, or concrete implication.
```

A feature becomes meaningful only when the reader can connect it to progress, reduced risk, saved effort, quality, or control.

Do not invent precision. If a quantified outcome lacks evidence, use an honest qualitative claim or label the proof gap.

## Calls to action

- Start with a verb.
- Name what happens next.
- Keep identical actions labeled identically.
- Distinguish different commitment levels.
- Avoid `Submit`, `OK`, `Click here`, and repeated bare `Learn more`.
- In confirmations, repeat the actual consequence.

Examples:

- `Start a free trial`
- `Book a product demo`
- `Download the migration guide`
- `Delete project`
- `View billing documentation`

## Voice and tone

Recon the existing voice first. Keep one product voice; adjust tone by stakes:

- warm for onboarding and success
- neutral for routine work
- calm and direct for errors
- explicit for destructive, security, privacy, or data-loss situations

Use active voice, familiar words, and short sentences. Humor must not obscure action, translate poorly, or appear in high-stakes moments.

## Interface writing

- Buttons name actions; links name destinations.
- Toggle labels describe the on state.
- Placeholder text shows a format; it never replaces a visible label.
- Empty states orient, explain how content appears, and offer one relevant next step.
- Errors say what failed and how to recover, next to the failure.
- Success confirms the result and any known next step.
- Loading copy should not promise timing the system cannot guarantee.
- Destructive dialogs name the object and consequence.
- Notifications must not be the only place critical information appears.

## Forms

- Ask only what is needed now.
- Group by user intent, not database schema.
- Explain unusual or sensitive fields.
- Preserve autocomplete, paste, correct input type, and keyboard flow.
- Validate in a way that supports recovery.
- Retain user input after recoverable failure.
- Do not disable submission so aggressively that users cannot discover what needs correction.
- Make privacy and consent choices explicit.

## Localization resilience

- Do not concatenate translated sentence fragments.
- Allow labels and body copy to expand.
- Avoid direction-specific language when interfaces support RTL.
- Use locale-aware dates, numbers, currencies, and pluralization.
- Do not embed important words inside images.
- Keep idioms, cultural references, and wordplay intentional.

## Copy review

Check:

- factual support
- audience relevance
- message hierarchy
- terminology consistency
- action clarity
- recovery
- tone by stakes
- scanability
- translation risk
- fit at representative widths

Report the root problem, not every repeated symptom.
