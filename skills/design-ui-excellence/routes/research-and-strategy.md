# Research & Strategy Route

Use this route when the requested deliverable is understanding, framing, prioritization, a design brief, or a strategic recommendation rather than a finished interface.

Do not use it as a substitute for building when the user already supplied a sufficiently complete brief.

## Workflow

### 1. Name the decision

State the concrete decision this work must enable. Examples:

- Which audience and problem should the site prioritize?
- What should the first version include?
- Why are visitors failing to understand or trust the offer?
- Which design direction best supports the desired market position?
- Which user flow should be redesigned first?

Research without a pending decision becomes documentation theater.

### 2. Inventory available evidence

Inspect user-provided research, analytics, support themes, sales objections, current product behavior, existing content, competitors, and prior attempts.

Separate:

- **Fact** — directly supported by evidence.
- **Inference** — a reasoned interpretation of evidence.
- **Assumption** — necessary but currently unverified.
- **Unknown** — material information that cannot be established.

Never turn an assumption into a persona fact or market claim.

### 3. Frame the audience and job

Describe the primary audience by context, motivation, constraints, current alternative, desired outcome, and awareness level. Prefer behavior and situation over fictional biography.

When multiple audiences exist, choose a primary audience for the requested artifact and state what secondary audiences lose.

### 4. Build a reference field

Study more than direct competitors:

- direct products establish category expectations
- adjacent products reveal useful interaction and content patterns
- editorial, cultural, architectural, or physical references can establish visual character
- anti-references make unwanted defaults explicit

Record what each reference contributes. Do not collapse the research into “make it like X.”

### 5. Define the strategic choice

A useful strategy excludes alternatives. State:

- target audience
- priority problem or job
- proposed position or experience principle
- reason to believe
- primary action
- explicit non-goals
- material tradeoffs

### 6. Define success and evidence

Translate “better,” “modern,” or “higher converting” into observable outcomes. Choose only relevant signals, such as comprehension, task completion, qualified signups, reduced abandonment, content discovery, support reduction, or implementation consistency.

Do not manufacture baselines. Mark measurement dependencies.

### 7. Recommend the next route

End with the route that should consume the research:

- website
- landing page
- product interface
- campaign
- brand direction
- design system
- content and SEO
- prototype and variants

## Deliverable

Produce a concise decision document with:

1. decision to make
2. evidence and gaps
3. audience and context
4. problem or opportunity
5. strategic choice and tradeoffs
6. success criteria
7. assumptions and risks
8. recommended next route

Use [`../templates/design-brief.md`](../templates/design-brief.md) when the work spans more than one session or contributor.

## Failure patterns

- fictional personas unsupported by research
- competitor feature matrices with no decision attached
- collecting inspiration without extracting principles
- confusing user requests with user needs
- treating “everyone” as an audience
- using surveys for questions that require observation or interviews
- describing a problem while silently prescribing a preferred solution
- producing a long report that does not alter the next design decision
