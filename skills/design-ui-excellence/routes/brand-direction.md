# Brand & Art Direction Route

Use this route for visual identity direction, brand worlds, moodboards, look-and-feel studies, image systems, reference-inspired concepts, and the visual foundation for a website or campaign.

This route creates a defensible visual language. It does not replace positioning, trademark review, or professional identity production when those are required.

## Start from identity, not effects

Establish:

- audience
- position and promise
- desired perception
- personality tensions, such as precise but human
- category conventions to preserve
- category conventions to reject
- existing recognition assets
- contexts the identity must survive
- constraints and exclusions

Translate adjectives into observable choices. “Premium” might mean disciplined typography, controlled pacing, tactile material, fewer claims, and strong product photography; it does not automatically mean black backgrounds and serif display type.

## Study references safely

For every supplied reference, separate:

### Reusable grammar

- hierarchy
- density
- proportion
- rhythm
- palette relationships
- material or image treatment
- typography roles
- motion character
- relationship between subject and negative space

### Distinctive identity

- names and marks
- slogans and exact copy
- proprietary assets
- recognizable characters or products
- signature letterforms
- unusual compositions
- distinctive motifs or choreography

Use the first category as learning. Do not reproduce the second.

Similarity labels are subjective planning tools, not measured facts. When the user asks for close inspiration, materially change identity, subject, meaning, composition, and signature motifs.

## Explore directions

When direction is unresolved, produce three directions from genuinely different strategic or visual positions. For each:

- name
- thesis
- why it fits
- signature choices
- reference principles
- hero expression
- risks and cost
- what it deliberately avoids

Do not present three colorways of the same composition. Hold the brief constant and vary the design answer.

When the user already specified a direction, do not manufacture alternatives. Refine and document the chosen system.

## Lock the system

Once direction is selected, create a style contract covering:

- brand thesis
- visual dials
- palette roles and legal pairings
- typography roles
- composition and grid
- shape and surface language
- asset and icon rules
- photography or illustration treatment
- motion character
- voice
- signature moment
- explicit do-not list

A style contract should contain decisions that another project could plausibly make differently.

## Validate recognition and range

Test the direction in at least the representative contexts required by the brief, such as:

- small mark or favicon
- navigation and UI chrome
- hero composition
- content-heavy section
- mobile crop
- social/share image
- light and dark environments
- motion and static fallback

Check that the system remains recognizable without relying on one effect and that generated assets remain in the same visual world.

## Handoff

Deliver the selected direction, rationale, contract, asset inventory, provenance gaps, representative applications, and unresolved identity or legal risks. Reroute to website, campaign, or design system for implementation.
