# Composition: organize the complete game frame

## Start with masses and a reading path

Before drawing borders, divide the frame into gameplay subject, primary decision,
context, persistent status, and transient feedback. Make the primary decision
obvious through grouping, placement, size, and value—not simultaneous maximum
contrast everywhere. Describe the intended reading sequence in one sentence.

Use a rough grayscale blockout with realistic content. Squint or inspect a small
thumbnail to detect competing masses. Then return to actual play size to check
text. A panel can be visually quiet at thumbnail scale while retaining excellent
readability at full size; these are different tests.

## Three composition structures

| Structure | Useful when | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| World-led peripheral HUD | Watching movement or combat matters most | Status pushed too far into peripheral vision |
| Illustration + decision column | Character/build choice has expressive value | Art crowds comparison data or focus |
| Context canvas + bounded inspector | Strategy, maps, research | Too many simultaneous panels hide cause and effect |

These are starting structures, not templates every game must use. Break symmetry
intentionally: a large portrait can balance a compact action column. Anchor the
relationship to a stable composition region rather than attaching everything to
opposite extreme edges of an ultrawide display.

## Hierarchy without giant widgets

Assign density by task. Fast decisions need a short stable scan; considered
planning can use compact aligned rows and progressive detail. Group related
values spatially before enclosing them in another card. Repeated thin dividers,
baseline alignment, and whitespace can communicate structure without a nest of
boxes. Avoid a web-dashboard card grid merely because it is easy to generate.

Align numerals by decimal or unit where comparison benefits. Keep labels close to
the values they explain. Position consequence previews near the action they
qualify. Reserve a stable lane for prompts instead of letting tooltip expansion
move the active target.

## Adapt composition, not only scale

Define what reflows, what stays fixed in logical units, and what may collapse.
An inventory may replace simultaneous comparison columns with a switchable detail
pane on narrow screens; it must not silently remove comparison. On ultrawide,
extend atmosphere or map context before stretching text into long lines.

Preserve input reach and selection after a reflow. Re-evaluate focus neighbors
and scrolling. Distinguish an OS safe rectangle from your designed comfort inset.
Decoration may bleed to the edge while decisions stay inside a readable region.

## Repair order

First correct overlapping or hidden content. Next correct the reading path and
large panel proportions. Then correct spacing, baseline, and typographic rhythm.
Only then refine strokes, corner ornament, textures, and animation. Adding detail
to a weak composition makes it more expensive to repair later.
