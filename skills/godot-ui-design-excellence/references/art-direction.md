# Art direction: turn identity into a repeatable visual grammar

## Author a system, not a mood adjective

Define the interface's relationship to the fictional world. Is it an annotated
field journal, a civic instrument, a ritual, a tactical overlay, or a deliberately
external information layer? A metaphor should organize decisions, not force every
control to look like a physical object.

Specify five rules with consequences:

1. **Composition:** where information gathers, what stays visually open, and how
   full-screen menus differ from the HUD.
2. **Typography:** how display, decision, explanation, numeric data, and prompts
   differ without looking like unrelated brands.
3. **Shape and material:** which repeated forms represent interaction, grouping,
   status, and noninteractive atmosphere.
4. **Attention:** where strong contrast, saturation, scale, and animation are
   permitted—and where they are deliberately withheld.
5. **Motion:** a small family of directional, timing, and interruption rules.

Riot's Hextech article is useful as a method: its designers connect shape,
material, interaction hierarchy, and movement to a consistent fictional language.
The transferable lesson is that relationship, not its particular gold/blue palette.

## Make alternatives structurally different

When exploration is requested, offer two or three directions with different
composition, typography, and material logic—not the same panel with alternate
accent colors. For example, a cartographic RPG could choose editorial atlas,
hand-annotated expedition record, or austere scientific instrument. Explain which
player experience each supports and what it costs in readability or production.

Commit one representative screen before manufacturing an entire library. The
screen should contain a real hard case: long labels, nested information, selected
and unavailable actions, or a busy gameplay background. An empty title screen
alone does not establish a workable combat or inventory system.

## Define ornament rules

Give decorative marks a limited job: frame the primary decision, identify a faction,
separate sections, or establish material character. Create a detail budget:
quiet text interior, restrained boundary treatment, distinctive focal ornament.
If texture exists at equal intensity everywhere, there is no hierarchy.

Do not mistake symmetry for professionalism or asymmetry for originality. Choose
balance around the gameplay subject. Optical alignment may differ slightly from
numeric alignment; record intentional exceptions rather than gradually drifting.

## Turn identity into implementation constraints

Name semantic tokens such as `surface.inspection`, `text.secondary`,
`state.focus`, `stroke.divider`, and `motion.panel_enter`. Component variants
represent roles, not arbitrary colors. Give the asset artist separate guidance
for silhouette, border thickness at final display size, alpha, and safe text area.

Record an approved target and rejection examples. A new panel must belong to the
same family when viewed beside the old one, not merely match its hex colors.
Do not require identical density for a strategic ledger and a dramatic result
screen; explain how both use the same grammar differently.

Acceptance: the screen has a recognizable identity with its decorative layer
muted, and the decorative layer strengthens that identity without obscuring the
player's task. This is a design review criterion, not a claim of objective beauty.
